\chapter{Failure analysis handbook}
\label{ch:failure-modes}

This chapter is a symptom-first field guide. Start with what the bench,
production line, or fleet reports. Preserve the failing state until its evidence
has been captured, then use the same workflow for every incident. Distinguish
observation, correlation, hypothesis, leading mechanism, and confirmed mechanism
(after controlled confirmation). Do not call the last surviving hypothesis the confirmed
mechanism without controlled reproduction, swap testing, stress dependence, or
physical evidence. Do not say ``data proves'' until confirmation exists. For
each symptom, state access (black-box versus engineering), the decision
unlocked, and the earliest production or fleet control
(\Cref{sec:tree-evidence-block,sec:interview-access-levels}).

\paragraph{Failure-analysis output categories.}
\label{sec:fa-output-categories}
Every FA result should land in one bucket before the case closes:

\begin{description}
\item[Design issue] Fix architecture or derate.
\item[Process issue] Fix manufacturing or assembly.
\item[Supplier issue] Fix incoming quality or supplier process.
\item[Test escape] Improve detection (ATP, sample, SPC, telemetry).
\item[Unknown mechanism] Continue investigation with an owner and interim
      control.
\end{description}

Connect FA back into qualification. After corrective action, name what changed:
requirement, qualification plan, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process
(\Cref{sec:tree-qual-evidence,sec:ladder-feedback}).

\begin{dectree}
Observe
  |
Scope (unit / lot / vendor / site / fleet)
  |
Time behavior (sudden / gradual / intermittent)
  |
Changing margin ledger
  |
Measure / isolate (name access level)
  |
Correct
  |
Recurrence control + FA output category
\end{dectree}

\begin{dectree}
Escalation
  |
Contain if population can grow
  |
Bench confirm at named plane
  |
FA / DPA when mechanism unknown
  |
CAPA + ATP / SPC / telemetry update
  |
Fleet monitor until burn-down
\end{dectree}

\begin{dectree}
Failure onset
  |
Sudden?
  |-- config / handling / firmware / mechanical / power event
  |
Gradual?
  |-- aging / drift / margin erosion / contamination / cal movement
  |
Prioritize measurements (priors, not conclusions)
\end{dectree}

Choose each measurement for its ability to separate competing hypotheses. The
debugging pyramid in \Cref{sec:debug-pyramid}, the power-versus-signal fork in
\Cref{sec:debug-fork}, the fleet router in \Cref{tab:fleet-triage}, and the
wall-chart trees in \Cref{app:decision-trees} provide the same method at
different scales.

\begin{description}
\item[Power loss] Split launch-power loss from coupling, connector, MUX, fiber,
      monitor, and receiver-plane errors (\Cref{sec:fm-power-loss}).
\item[BER increase] Separate a shifted waterfall from a BER floor, then check
      power, eye quality, noise, timing, and spectrum
      (\Cref{sec:fm-ber-increase,sec:fm-ber-floor}).
\item[Eye closure or low ER] Split bandwidth, drive, bias, reflection,
      dispersion, and resonance alignment
      (\Cref{sec:fm-eye-closure,sec:fm-low-er}).
\item[Lane imbalance] Split source, modulator or ring, filter or MUX, fiber
      attach, driver, and receiver (\Cref{sec:fm-lane-imbalance}).
\item[Temperature sensitivity] Track power, wavelength, lock error, TEC or heater
      headroom, receiver margin, and package stress (\Cref{sec:fm-temperature}).
\item[Wavelength drift] Split source movement from filter, ring, TEC, and
      control-loop movement (\Cref{sec:fm-wavelength-drift}).
\item[Intermittent bursts] Preserve counters before reseating. Check connector
      contamination, ORL, supply noise, lock state, and weak attach
      (\Cref{sec:fm-intermittent}).
\item[Yield drop] Clear the tester, then split lot, site, process step, assembly,
      firmware, and calibration (\Cref{sec:fm-yield-drop}).
\end{description}

The cases below provide the detailed measurements and corrective actions. Use the
failure and debug callouts in Chapters 3--9 as shorter versions of the same
method. Earlier chapters own the mechanism physics. This chapter owns the order
of operations.

\section{Power loss}
\label{sec:fm-power-loss}

\begin{dectree}
Power loss
  |
External meter vs CMIS
  |
Plane walk (source -> coupling -> MUX -> connector -> Rx)
  |
LIV at failing T
  |
Decision: source / path / monitor
  |
Recurrence: earliest ATP power check
\end{dectree}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
Received power or OMA falls at one or more lanes. BER may remain stable at first,
then rise as receiver margin is consumed. The module monitor and an external
power meter may disagree.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
Launch power can fall because the laser is disabled, thermally rolled over, or
aged. Power can disappear after the source through modulator loss, coupling
shift, MUX loss, fiber bend, connector contamination, or a wrong reference-plane
calibration. A drifting monitor photodiode can report loss that does not exist.

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compare CMIS Tx and Rx power, external power at the faceplate, bias current,
      and case temperature. Disagreement identifies the first suspect plane.
\item Walk optical power plane by plane with a known source and calibrated meter.
      Do not change bias or equalization while locating the loss.
\item Rerun LIV at the failing temperature. A moved LIV raises $P(\mathrm{source})$;
      a stable LIV raises $P(\mathrm{path\ or\ monitor})$ until confirmed. Do not treat
      either as a confirmed mechanism before controlled confirmation.
\item Preserve telemetry, then inspect connectors before cleaning
      (\Cref{sec:fm-contamination}). Measure insertion loss and ORL. Use a golden
      fiber and module swap to separate field plant from module.
\item For a weak lane, compare sibling lanes and per-lane coupling. Lot or lane
      clustering points toward assembly or MUX variation.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
Repair the first plane where power diverges. Correct calibration or monitor
coefficients before changing source bias. Add a power check at the earliest
production plane that can catch the signature and retain golden-path baselines
for fleet comparison.
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{BER increase: waterfall shift or floor}
\label{sec:fm-ber-increase}

\begin{dectree}
BER up
  |
Power held?
  |-- NO  --> power path (\Cref{sec:fm-power-loss})
  |-- YES --> waterfall
              |-- shift --> sensitivity / OMA / IL
              |-- floor --> RIN / MPI / noise / crosstalk
  |
Decision + ATP / telemetry control
\end{dectree}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
Pre-FEC BER rises, but the first task is to determine whether the whole BER
waterfall moved to higher received power or whether it stopped improving at a
horizontal floor. A waterfall is BER versus received power from a VOA sweep at
a named reference plane. One operating-point BER is not enough to classify the
failure.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
A shifted waterfall points toward lost power, receiver sensitivity, eye
closure, timing, or dispersion: the link still responds to more photons, but
the power needed for a given BER has moved. A floor points toward
signal-proportional noise, reflection, crosstalk, or bursty impairments: past
a point, more power does not help because noise grows with the signal. The
distinction prevents a power fix from being applied to a noise-limited link.
FEC error timing further splits the floor and shift cases: randomly sprinkled
errors fit Gaussian or steady RIN; clustered bursts fit MPI, connector
intermittents, or shared supply and clock events.

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sweep received power and plot BER rather than recording one operating
      point. Confirm whether the curve shifted, floored, or both (a soft floor
      on top of a shift is common).
\item If the waterfall shifted, compare received OMA, TDECQ, receiver
      sensitivity, equalizer taps, wavelength, and temperature with the golden
      baseline. Golden-swap Tx and Rx to assign ownership.
\item If the curve floors, follow \Cref{sec:fm-ber-floor} and split intrinsic
      RIN, electrical noise, ORL, MPI, and crosstalk.
\item Use FEC error timing and lane correlation to separate random noise from
      bursts and shared disturbances. Preserve counters before reseating if the
      pattern is intermittent.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
Restore the margin ledger that moved, then repeat the full BER sweep at loaded
corners. Store waterfall shape, not only pass/fail BER, so later fleet changes
can be classified without guessing. The interview study treatment of these
shapes is in \Cref{sec:interview-waterfall}.
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{BER floor}
\label{sec:fm-ber-floor}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
Pre-FEC BER improves as you increase transmit or received power, then stops
improving and flattens at a constant floor regardless of how much more power
you add. The FEC histogram may still look random (steady RIN) or bursty
(MPI, intermittents); the floor shape alone does not decide that split.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
A BER floor means additional received power no longer removes the dominant
impairment. That is a diagnostic pattern, not one mechanism. RIN can create a
floor when signal-proportional intensity noise dominates the receiver budget:
$\sigma_\mathrm{RIN} \propto I$, so $Q$ can saturate at
$Q_\mathrm{max} = 1/\sqrt{\mathrm{RIN}_\mathrm{lin} \cdot \mathrm{BW}}$ under a
dominant-RIN model (\Cref{sec:rin}). Do not define every floor as RIN-limited.
Other leading mechanisms include multipath interference (MPI), bias-rail noise
that converts to equivalent intensity noise (\Cref{sec:laser-drivers}),
pattern-dependent distortion or residual ISI, crosstalk, timing or CDR limits,
and DSP or equalization limits.

MPI may produce deterministic interference, power-independent floors, pattern
sensitivity, environmental sensitivity, or time-correlated errors depending on
coherence, path delay, motion, and modulation. Useful evidence includes ORL
dependence, delay-related structure on an ESA, aggressor dependence, pattern
dependence, thermal or mechanical sensitivity, and FEC error timing. Do not treat
``bursty FEC histogram'' alone as proof of MPI.

\paragraph{Required access.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Black-box / bookended:} attenuation sweep, pre-FEC BER and FEC
      timing, Tx/Rx power telemetry, temperature, host/module swap, lane remap.
\item \textbf{Engineering access:} intrinsic RIN, product-board RIN,
      $\mathrm{RIN}_x\mathrm{OMA}$ at a named ORL, optical eye, controlled
      reflector, ESA, optical breakout.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Measurements and isolation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Confirm the floor exists: sweep received power (or Tx OMA) at a named
      reference plane and plot BER vs.\ power. A floor appears as a horizontal
      asymptote.
\item Where engineering access exists, bisect optical vs.\ electrical RIN: quiet
      SMU (intrinsic) versus product bias board. If the floor moves, the electrical
      path is injecting noise (\Cref{sec:laser-drivers}).
\item Sweep ORL with a controlled reflector. If the floor worsens with lower ORL,
      the path is feedback-sensitive. Check isolator, connector, and fiber-attach
      cleanliness.
\item Gather MPI evidence (ORL, delay structure, pattern, thermal/mechanical, FEC
      timing). Treat MPI as a leading mechanism until confirmed.
\item Compare measured $\mathrm{RIN}_x\mathrm{OMA}$ at the stated ORL against the
      named PMD revision and ATP limit (for example $-136$~dB/Hz at 17.1~dB ORL for
      a cited DR-class clause~\cite{rinspec}). State the plane and condition.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
If intrinsic RIN is confirmed as the limiter, replace or derate. If electrical
RIN, fix the bias supply. If ORL-driven, clean or replace connectors and verify
isolator function. If MPI is confirmed from multiple reflections, reduce mated
interfaces or improve their ORL. Update the earliest economical control (ATP,
sampled audit, SPC, or telemetry), not every deep FA measurement.
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{Low extinction ratio}
\label{sec:fm-low-er}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
Transmitter OMA looks low on the DCA even though average power is in range. TDECQ
may or may not fail depending on how the reference equalizer compensates.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
Linear extinction ratio is $\mathrm{ER}_\mathrm{lin}=P_1/P_0$ (for PAM4 outer
levels use $P_3/P_0$). In decibels,
$\mathrm{ER}_\mathrm{dB}=10\log_{10}(\mathrm{ER}_\mathrm{lin})$ and
$\mathrm{ER}_\mathrm{lin}=10^{\mathrm{ER}_\mathrm{dB}/10}$. Low ER means the
off level is too high or the on level is too low. In an EML, ER is set by EAM
reverse bias; in a DML, by modulation depth relative to threshold.

An idealized receiver OMA penalty for finite ER is
\[
\mathrm{PP}_\mathrm{dB}
=
10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{ER}_\mathrm{lin}+1}{\mathrm{ER}_\mathrm{lin}-1}\right).
\]
At 10~dB ER the penalty is $\sim$0.87~dB; at 6~dB ER it rises to $\sim$2.2~dB
(\Cref{sec:sensitivity}). This is an idealized receiver-penalty model, not a
measured compliance quantity such as TDECQ.

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Measure ER on the DCA (outer OMA / average power, or directly from the
      histogram levels). Compare against the PMD limit.
\item \textbf{EML:} Sweep EAM bias. ER should peak at the optimal bias point; if
      the curve has shifted (aged EAM), the operating point needs recalibration.
      Check EAM bias DAC code vs.\ datasheet.
\item \textbf{DML:} Check bias current vs.\ LIV. If bias is close to threshold,
      modulation depth is limited. Increase bias (but watch thermal rollover and
      RIN).
\item \textbf{MZM:} Check quadrature bias. If the MZM has drifted off quadrature
      ($V_\pi/2$ point), extinction degrades. Log the bias-control loop error
      signal; a saturated loop indicates drift beyond correction range.
\item \textbf{Ring:} Check resonance alignment. If the ring is detuned from the
      laser wavelength, extinction drops. Monitor the thermal tuner current and
      wavelength-lock error.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
Recalibrate the modulator operating point. For EML aging, update the EAM bias
setpoint in firmware or flag the module for replacement if the absorption curve has
shifted beyond the correctable range. For MZM drift, verify the bias controller and
its monitor PD. For rings, retune or check for neighbor thermal crosstalk
(\Cref{sec:thermal-xtalk}).
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{Lane imbalance}
\label{sec:fm-lane-imbalance}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
In a multi-lane module (DR4, FR4, DR8), one or more lanes show significantly
different OMA, TDECQ, or pre-FEC BER compared with siblings in the same module.
The weak lane may be marginal or failing while others are healthy.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
Multi-lane modules share a substrate, laser array (or CW-WDM source), and thermal
environment. Lane-to-lane variation comes from: (1)~die-level non-uniformity in the
laser or modulator array (threshold, slope, $V_\pi$, coupling), (2)~packaging
variation in fiber-array alignment (one channel of the FAU slightly misaligned),
(3)~driver or TIA channel mismatch on the electronic IC, or (4)~thermal gradient
across the die (edge lanes hotter or cooler than center lanes).

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Measure all lanes: OMA, ER, TDECQ, RIN, wavelength. Identify the outlier.
\item \textbf{Is it optical or electrical?} Swap the electrical lane assignment
      (if the host supports lane remapping) or test the suspect optical lane with a
      known-good driver channel. If the problem follows the optical path, it is
      laser/modulator/fiber-attach. If it follows the electrical channel, it is
      driver/TIA.
\item \textbf{Check coupling.} Measure per-lane fiber-coupled power with an
      integrating sphere or power meter array. A single weak lane with normal LIV
      suggests FAU misalignment.
\item \textbf{Thermal map.} Use an IR camera or CMIS per-lane monitors (if
      available) to check for hot spots. Edge lanes near the package wall or near a
      TEC boundary may run hotter.
\item \textbf{Time-growing imbalance.} A lane that worsens over time raises the
      probability of lane-specific source, modulator, receiver, coupling,
      packaging, or control degradation. Do not immediately call it laser aging.
      Where engineering access exists, compare LIV, bias, lock error, and coupling
      at $t_0$ and now before naming the mechanism.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Required access.}
Black-box path: per-lane power/BER/FEC, host lane remap, sibling comparison,
temperature. Engineering-access path: LIV, optical eye, TDECQ, per-lane coupling,
bias sweeps, FAU inspection.

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
FAU misalignment is a manufacturing escape; tighten incoming inspection or
first-article coupling specs. Thermal gradient: redesign TEC zoning or derate the
hot lane. Confirmed source wear-out: flag the lot and check burn-in screening
effectiveness. Driver mismatch: work with the IC supplier on channel-to-channel
gain flatness.
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{Wavelength drift}
\label{sec:fm-wavelength-drift}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
In a WDM system, one or more channels walk off the ITU grid or the ring/filter
passband. BER degrades as the channel moves off the receiver filter or MUX/DEMUX
passband. In ring-modulator CPO, this manifests as sudden unlock of the wavelength
control loop.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
Laser wavelength moves with temperature and bias current. If the TEC or
wavelength-locker servo cannot track, the channel walks off its assigned slot.
In microring systems, resonance also moves strongly with temperature, and
neighbor heaters create thermal crosstalk that pushes adjacent channels
(\Cref{sec:locking-techniques,sec:thermal-xtalk}).

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Measure wavelength on an OSA or wavemeter. Compare to the target grid.
\item \textbf{Laser-side:} Check TEC current. If saturated (at max or min drive),
      the thermal load exceeds TEC capacity. Check case temperature and airflow.
\item \textbf{Locker-side:} Read the wavelength-locker error signal. A healthy loop
      holds near zero; drift means the servo is losing lock. Check locker etalon
      alignment and PD balance.
\item \textbf{Ring CPO:} Check the ring thermal tuner DAC code. If it has railed
      (max heater power), the ring cannot reach the target wavelength. Check for
      neighbor heating (all adjacent lanes at full traffic and max case $T$).
\item \textbf{Aging:} If drift is progressive over weeks/months, suspect laser mode
      hop or gradual TEC degradation (reduced $\Delta T$ capacity).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
TEC saturation: reduce case temperature (improve airflow or liquid cooling) or
derate the laser operating current. Ring unlock: increase heater headroom in the
design, reduce thermal crosstalk with layout changes, or shift the CW-WDM source
grid to re-center the ring tuning range. Aging: schedule preventive replacement
(ELSFP hot-swap, \Cref{sec:elsfp}).
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{Eye closure (high TDECQ)}
\label{sec:fm-eye-closure}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
TDECQ exceeds the PMD limit even though average power and ER look acceptable. The
DCA eye appears compressed or distorted after the reference equalizer is applied.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
TDECQ measures how much noise the transmitter can tolerate before BER exceeds the
FEC threshold, relative to an ideal transmitter (\Cref{sec:tdecq}). High TDECQ
means the equalized eye is poor. Common causes: (1)~insufficient EO bandwidth
(modulator or driver roll-off), (2)~poor level linearity (RLM $<$ 0.95;
driver or modulator compression), (3)~pattern-dependent effects (ISI from
bandwidth limit, reflections, or impedance mismatch), (4)~chromatic dispersion on
FR-class fiber eating into the margin.

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Inspect the raw (unequalized) eye on the DCA. Is it bandwidth-limited
      (rounded transitions), compressed (uneven levels), or noisy (RIN/jitter)?
\item \textbf{Bandwidth:} Measure EO $S_{21}$ of the modulator (if accessible) or
      the combined Tx path. Compare to the Nyquist frequency. If 3-dB BW is below
      Nyquist, bandwidth is the limiter.
\item \textbf{RLM:} Compute relative level mismatch from the DCA histogram. If
      $\mathrm{RLM} < 0.95$, the PAM4 levels are unevenly spaced. Check driver
      linearity (swept DAC code) and modulator transfer function (bias sweep).
\item \textbf{Reflections:} Check $S_{11}$ of the RF path (driver to modulator)
      and the optical return loss. Reflections cause post-cursor ISI the FFE
      cannot fully cancel.
\item \textbf{Dispersion:} Measure TDECQ with and without the test fiber (TECQ vs.\
      TDECQ). If TDECQ is significantly worse than TECQ, dispersion is the marginal
      contributor. Check Tx wavelength and chirp against the fiber length and
      dispersion coefficient.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
Bandwidth: upgrade driver or modulator (higher-BW die or peaking network). RLM:
tune driver pre-emphasis (DAC levels for each PAM4 symbol). Reflections: fix
wirebond, impedance discontinuity, or connector. Dispersion: tighten wavelength
tolerance or shorten the fiber (re-route).

\section{Thermal runaway}
\label{sec:fm-thermal-runaway}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
Module temperature rises continuously until shutdown (CMIS over-temperature alarm)
or until the laser degrades catastrophically. May start subtly: BER creeps up as
case $T$ rises during traffic ramp or with neighbor-lane loading.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
In a faceplate pluggable, double-digit-watt module power must leave through the
cage and heatsink~\cite{switchthermal}. If airflow is blocked, the cage is
overloaded, or the TEC inside the module is fighting a losing battle against
junction temperature, the thermal loop runs away. In CPO, the optical engine sits
on the switch substrate beside the ASIC; cooling-path faults concentrate heat on
the source and ring controls~\cite{cpocooling}. Both sources are vendor or
research orientation, so the product thermal model remains authoritative.

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Read CMIS module temperature and compare to the module's rated case $T$
      range. If case $T$ exceeds the max, the system cooling is inadequate.
\item \textbf{Check TEC current.} A TEC at max drive current is saturated; it
      cannot pump more heat. The junction temperature is higher than the case $T$
      suggests.
\item \textbf{Measure LIV at temperature.} If threshold rises and slope drops
      steeply with $T$, the laser is near thermal rollover (\Cref{sec:laser-aging}).
      The operating point may be marginal.
\item \textbf{Neighbor loading.} Bring all lanes and neighbor modules to full
      traffic simultaneously. If the problem only appears under full-cage load, the
      thermal design margin is insufficient.
\item \textbf{Airflow audit.} Inspect the faceplate for blocked vents, incorrect
      fan speed, or missing blanking panels (bypass airflow). For liquid-cooled
      systems (CPO, XPO), check coolant flow rate and inlet temperature.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
System-level: improve airflow, lower ambient, or reduce module count per cage.
Module-level: derate the laser (lower bias current reduces self-heating) or switch
to a lower-power module style (LPO instead of retimed, \Cref{sec:conditioning}).
CPO: ensure the cold-plate thermal interface material (TIM) is intact and the
liquid loop meets flow-rate spec. Long-term: specify a tighter thermal class in the
laser requirements (\Cref{sec:laser-reqs}).
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{Intermittent failures}
\label{sec:fm-intermittent}

\begin{dectree}
Intermittent / burst
  |
Preserve state (do not reseat yet)
  |
Scope time + change history
  |
ORL / connector / supply / lock / attach
  |
Decision: contain / clean / redesign / ATP dwell
  |
Recurrence: dwell + FEC histograms
\end{dectree}


\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
Links flap, lose lock, or show bursts of FEC errors while average power and a
short bench BER test look normal. The symptom may clear after reseating, cooling,
or restarting firmware.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
Intermittent faults come from contacts, reflections, weak optical or electrical
attach, supply noise, wavelength-lock loss, firmware state, or environmental
stress. Reseating can remove the evidence by cleaning a contact, changing fiber
stress, or resetting a state machine.

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Freeze CMIS state, FEC error timing, LOS or LOL history, temperatures,
      rails, lock error, and neighbor activity before touching hardware.
\item Scope the pattern across lane, module, tray, rack, lot, and firmware
      revision. Shared timing points toward power, cooling, firmware, or a shared
      source.
\item Correlate bursts with Rx power, ORL, supply and clock spurs, lock-loop
      state, vibration, and temperature. Use trigger capture rather than averages.
\item Run controlled disturbance tests one at a time: connector motion, thermal
      ramp, neighbor load, rail load, and firmware state transition.
\item Repeat on a golden unit and fixture. If the fault follows the unit, inspect
      fiber attach, solder, contacts, and control logs before destructive analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
Fix the confirmed contact, attach, supply, lock, or firmware cause. Add
event-triggered telemetry and a production stress that reproduces the fault.
Keep intermittent and no-fault-found RMA codes separate from laser wear-out.
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{Connector contamination}
\label{sec:fm-contamination}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
Intermittent link failures, burst errors, or elevated BER that clears after
reseating or cleaning a connector. Rx power may fluctuate or show sudden drops.
Often affects one direction of a duplex link.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
A particle of dust on an MT, LC, or MPO ferrule endface scatters and absorbs
light, raising insertion loss and back-reflection (lowering ORL). Debris in the
core zone can cause large loss even when most of the ferrule looks clean.
Elevated ORL feeds back into the laser and raises RIN, causing burst errors
even when average power looks acceptable. In high-power CW-WDM and ELSFP systems,
trapped particles can burn onto the fiber endface and cause permanent damage.

\paragraph{Measurements and isolation.}
Preserve evidence before you disturb the mating:
\begin{dectree}
Preserve telemetry and failure history
  |
Photograph and inspect before disturbance
  |
Record contamination and damage
  |
Clean and re-inspect
  |
Measure insertion loss and ORL
  |
Retest BER and sensitivity
\end{dectree}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Capture CMIS/host counters, timestamps, Tx/Rx power, alarms, and which
      port failed before reseating or cleaning.
\item Photograph and inspect with a fiber-endface scope (200--400$\times$) before
      disturbance. Record particles in the core zone, scratches, pits, residue, and
      burn marks.
\item Clean and re-inspect (dry-click cleaner or lint-free wipe with IPA). If the
      endface still fails IEC 61300-3-35 zone criteria, replace the
      jumper~\cite{iec61300}.
\item Measure insertion loss and ORL across the mated pair at the named plane.
      Compare to the link-budget allocation (\Cref{sec:link-budget}).
\item Retest BER and sensitivity. Clearing after cleaning supports contamination as
      the leading mechanism; confirm with IL/ORL and watch for recurrence. Log
      connector location and date code.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
After evidence is preserved: clean, re-inspect, and verify IL/ORL and BER.
Preventive: dust caps on unused ports, ``inspect before connect'' in the service
runbook, sealed cassettes or trunk cables that minimize open-ferrule exposure. For
high-power paths (ELSFP, CW-WDM), burn damage requires replacement, not
re-cleaning. Track contamination RMAs as a distinct failure code (not ``laser
failure'') so FIT accounting stays honest (\Cref{sec:fleet-triage}).

\section{Yield drop}
\label{sec:fm-yield-drop}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
First-pass yield falls below its stable baseline. The loss may cluster on one ATP
row, lane, tester, shift, supplier lot, assembly site, or firmware revision.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
Process drift, incoming material variation, fiber-array alignment, die-attach or
solder change, tester or fixture drift, stale calibration, a software limit change,
or a guardband that no longer matches measurement spread.

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Contain suspect work in process and freeze tester software, limits, fixtures,
      and calibration records.
\item Build a Pareto by ATP row, lot, date code, site, tester, and shift. Do not
      average away a one-lane or one-station signature.
\item Run a golden unit across stations and the same failed unit on the reference
      bench. This clears the measurement system before supplier action begins.
\item Compare upstream wafer, die, assembly, and incoming data at the first point
      where good and bad populations separate.
\item Select failure analysis that distinguishes the remaining mechanisms, then
      verify the correction on a controlled lot and watch the field code.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
Restore the changed process or measurement input, add a statistical control at the
first observable point, revise ATP only with correlation data, and require a new
first-article check before releasing volume (\Cref{sec:supplier-exec}).
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\section{Aging, thermal response, and margin erosion}
\label{sec:fm-aging-thermal-router}

Use time scale and recovery to route the incident. A reversible shift during a
temperature or neighbor-load sweep is a thermal operating-point problem. A
baseline that moves over stress hours or field months is aging. A sudden
permanent step after a cycle points toward damage or an assembly defect, not
ordinary thermal response.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Return the unit to its starting temperature and operating point. Record
      whether power, wavelength, bias, TDECQ, and BER recover.
\item Compare with ship and pre-stress data. Permanent LIV, spectrum, or bias-curve
      movement supports aging or damage.
\item Repeat the temperature sweep with source, wavelength-selective element,
      receiver, and neighbors isolated in turn.
\item Update the power, noise, timing, spectral, and control ledgers
      (\Cref{sec:laser-margin-erosion}). Several small shifts can explain a BER
      failure even when each component remains inside its stand-alone limit.
\item Route reversible thermal loss to cooling, control, calibration, or derating.
      Route cumulative change to HTOL and life-model review. Route lot-clustered
      permanent steps to manufacturing failure analysis.
\end{enumerate}

The corrective action must restore margin at combined corners. A room-temperature
retest does not close a high-temperature incident, and one clean HTOL readout does
not explain a reversible lock failure.

\section{Temperature sensitivity}
\label{sec:fm-temperature}

\paragraph{Observed behavior.}
BER, TDECQ, optical power, or lock stability degrades during a case-temperature
ramp or under loaded-neighbor conditions. The unit may recover when cooled.

\paragraph{Likely hypotheses.}
Laser threshold and slope drift, wavelength movement, ring-resonance drift, TEC or
heater saturation, EAM or MZM bias error, receiver-noise rise, package stress, or a
thermal gradient that affects one lane.

\paragraph{Measurements, mechanism isolation, and confirmation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Record case temperature, external optical power, wavelength, bias, TEC or
      heater current, lock error, OMA, ER, TDECQ, and pre-FEC BER on one time axis.
\item Repeat with neighbors off and on. A shared shift points toward thermal or
      supply coupling; a lone lane points toward its local path.
\item Hold the source fixed and move the wavelength-selective element, then reverse
      the test. This splits laser drift from ring or filter drift.
\item Rerun LIV and receiver sensitivity hot and cold. Separate lost launch power
      from lost receiver margin.
\item Inspect package and cooling interfaces if optical and electrical blocks pass
      alone but fail in the assembled system.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Corrective action and recurrence control.}
Restore thermal headroom, correct calibration and control limits, reduce coupling,
or derate the operating point. Add the loaded-neighbor temperature ramp to the ATP
or design-validation plan that missed it.
What changed afterward? Name the update among requirement, qualification, ATP, SPC, telemetry, or supplier process, and the FA output category (\Cref{sec:fa-output-categories}).

\subsection{Reading the failure-analysis checklist}
\label{sec:fa-checklist-read}

\paragraph{Preserve.}
What volatile evidence will a reseat, reboot, clean, or retest destroy?
Snapshot CMIS, BER/FEC history, rails, temperature, firmware, fixture, and
time before any invasive action. Photograph endfaces before cleaning if
contamination is plausible. \textbf{Exit when} the failing-state pack is
filed. \textbf{Decision:} proceed to scope, or stop ops from ``just
reseating.'' \textbf{Risk if skipped:} the fail clears and you learn nothing.

\paragraph{Scope / classify / locate (short).}
Name the population (unit, lot, vendor, site, fleet) before instruments
(\Cref{sec:fleet-triage}). Record sudden versus gradual and whether cool-down
recovers. Name which ledger moved first: power, noise, timing, spectrum, or
control (\Cref{sec:interview-margin-ledgers}). \textbf{Exit when} population,
time class, and first-moving ledger are evidenced. \textbf{Risk if skipped:}
wrong containment width or the wrong instrument tour.

\paragraph{Falsify.}
Which single measurement best separates the leading hypotheses? Write expected
results before you run the test. \textbf{Exit when} one measurement kills or
promotes the leading set. \textbf{Decision:} move survivors to Confirm, or
reopen Locate margin. \textbf{Risk if skipped:} confirmation bias.

\paragraph{Confirm / correct (short).}
Confirm the fault follows the suspected block under swap or stress, then show
before/after margin at the original failing corner. \textbf{Exit when}
ownership is assigned and the fix restores that corner. \textbf{Risk if
skipped:} you ``fix'' the wrong block or ship a lab-only cure.

\paragraph{Prevent.}
Where can production or fleet monitoring catch recurrence earliest? Version an
ATP row, SPC limit, or alarm with owner and due date.
\textbf{Exit when} recurrence control is owned and the cohort is under watch.
\textbf{Decision:} close the incident, or monitor-only if rate and impact
justify it (\Cref{tab:interview-decisions}). \textbf{Risk if skipped:} you
solve one RMA and train the factory to recreate it.

\subsection{Why the steps occur in this order}

Preserve first because reseat and reboot destroy state. Scope next so
containment width matches the population. Classify and locate margin before
falsify so you pick the cheap separating test. Confirm before correct so you
do not ship a story. Prevent last so the factory and fleet catch the next
escape. Later steps must not compensate for a missing preserve pack or a
wrong scope.

\subsection{Learning summary}

\begin{description}
\item[Preserve] Snapshot before you touch it.
\item[Scope] One unit, lot, vendor, site, or fleet?
\item[Classify / locate] Time class and which ledger moved first?
\item[Falsify / confirm] Kill hypotheses; confirm the block owns the fail.
\item[Correct / prevent] Restore the corner; catch the next escape in ATP
      or telemetry.
\end{description}

\section{Interview and design review questions}
\label{sec:failure-analysis-design-review}

\paragraph{Scoping and evidence.}
\begin{itemize}
\item BER rose on one rack after a software rollout. What data do you preserve
      before reseating modules?
\item How would you decide whether a symptom belongs to one unit, one lot, one
      vendor, one site, or the fleet?
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Root-cause isolation.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Received power is unchanged but BER worsened. Which measurement do you make
      next, and which hypotheses does it separate?
\item How do you distinguish a BER waterfall shift from a BER floor?
\item BER degrades after thermal cycling. How do you separate reversible thermal
      response, calibration error, assembly damage, and long-term aging?
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Production and fleet action.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A golden unit fails only on one production station. What must be cleared
      before opening supplier failure analysis?
\item Which recurrence control belongs in ATP, and which belongs in fleet
      telemetry?
\item When is a no-fault-found return evidence of weak triage rather than a good
      unit?
\end{itemize}

\keyidea{A useful failure analysis starts with a symptom and ends with a new
control. Preserve the failing state, split shared from local behavior, clear the
measurement system, and choose one measurement that can falsify the leading
hypothesis. The corrective action is incomplete until production or fleet data
show that the same signature no longer escapes.}
